How it works

1

Switch on Active Protection and allow location “Always” and notifications. That's it.

2

Drive like normal

Use Maps, play music, leave your phone in the mount. SpotterLA runs in the background.

3

Hear the heads-up

A chime, then “Speed camera. Approaching. Limit 35.” Short enough to take in without looking down.

What you get

Voice alerts

Short and to the point. You hear the speed limit, so your eyes stay on the road.

Runs in the background

It uses Apple's geofencing, the same thing behind location-based reminders, so alerts come in while you drive.

Camera map

The map follows you as you drive, with each camera's speed limit right on its pin.

Your location stays on your phone

No account, no ads, no tracking. Your location is never sent to us or anyone else.

Easy on your battery

GPS doesn't stay on. Your iPhone wakes SpotterLA only when you're near a camera.

Updated as the city adds cameras

Camera locations come from LADOT's Speed Safety program. We add new ones as they go up.

Help you build the habit of driving the limit. If one day you don't need SpotterLA anymore, we've done our job.

Questions

Does SpotterLA work outside Los Angeles?

Not yet. It covers the City of Los Angeles speed camera program run by LADOT.

Do I need to keep the app open?

No. Turn on protection, set location to “Always,” and SpotterLA works in the background.

Will it drain my battery?

Not that you'd notice. Apple's geofencing wakes the app only near cameras, and GPS never stays on.

Is my location shared with anyone?

No. Your location never leaves your iPhone. See the privacy policy.

What happens when my free trial ends?

If you keep auto-renew on, your $9.99 yearly plan starts and alerts keep working. If you turn it off, we'll remind you the day before the trial ends, and alerts pause after it does.

Is this about dodging tickets?

No. A lot of these cameras are near schools, and we built SpotterLA because a quick heads-up gets people to slow down right where it matters. The locations are public, published by the City of LA. Please drive the speed limit.

Where do the speed limits come from?

From OpenStreetMap. If a camera's limit isn't clear, SpotterLA just says “Speed camera. Approaching.” Always go by the posted sign.

Always obey posted speed limits and don't handle your phone while driving. Camera locations come from LADOT and may be incomplete or out of date. Speed limits come from OpenStreetMap and may be missing or wrong, so always follow posted signs. SpotterLA is not affiliated with the City of Los Angeles.
